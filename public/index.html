How a 10-minute expedition works

One expedition a day, then the screen goes away. No ads, no in-app purchases, no endless feed.

Open a Room

You get a 4-digit code and a QR code. Your kid scans it with their own tablet or phone, even from Grandma’s house in another state.

Solve Three Locks

A short story sets the scene. Each lock gives the Scout the objects and the Keeper the rules. Talking is allowed. Showing your screen is not.

Talk Off-Screen

The chest opens with one question to chat about at dinner. Your kid can try a solo chest, and the report shows what they did, where it got tricky, and what the next mission practices.

Three locks, three kinds of thinking

Every puzzle is built by a generator and checked by a solver, so it always has exactly one answer, and neither player can find it alone.

The Detective

The Scout gets picture clues. The Keeper gets witness notes. Together you cross out suspects until one is left, and your kid picks the clue that rules each one out.

The Temple Cipher

The Scout sees glowing symbols. The Keeper reads the rule book: “If there are more red than blue…” Your kid predicts what comes next first, and in the last round you swap: the kid holds the rules.

Spatial Reasoning

The Blueprint Workshop

The Scout first guesses how the 3D model looks from the side, then spins it to check. The Keeper holds the blueprint. Find the 1–3 cubes that are off and fix the bridge.

A report built from real moves, not guesses

Four short blocks: what went well, where it got tricky (tied to the exact clue), one question for you to ask, and a next mission that really practices it. Your child is only ever compared with their own earlier moves.

Spatial skills are trainable: a meta-analysis of 217 studies found lasting gains that carry over to untrained tasks (Uttal et al., 2013). We don’t promise a higher IQ. We show you what changed.
